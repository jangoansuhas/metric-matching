# Scope decision after the first public source screen

**Written 2026-09-29 UTC, after the broad SQL/dbt frame's first two repositories were inspected.** This is a disclosed redesign, not a preregistration preceding those observations. No pair comparisons, method outputs, metric values, or human labels from either selected repository have been inspected. This note does not retroactively alter `public_pair_selection_freeze.md`, its queue, the dated source-screen amendment, or the two-row selection ledger.

## Decision

Pause the proposed SQL/catalog bridge for the primary SANER Industrial paper. The paper's primary method and Jaffle development evaluation concern published dbt Semantic Layer metrics and MetricFlow query provenance. Both repositories selected by the broader source-output rule contain **zero** declared MetricFlow metrics in their pinned YAML. The old selections remain the first two eligible projects **under that original broad rule**. Their successful source screens and adapter failures are descriptive evidence of an applicability boundary, not a test set for declaration-based matching. Do not call them ineligible, replace them by opening rank 3, or fold their SQL outputs into the primary metric denominator.

A source-only audit identified 17 aggregate outputs at rank 1 and 3 at rank 2 under its stated rule; these counts are provisional and are **not** frozen compiled-output inventories. The earlier screen's two and three outputs were lower bounds. The claim that the selected projects necessarily contain only four possible pairs is unsupported. Neither the provisional SQL counts nor the absence of metric declarations solves the missing human judgments or Industrial relevance.

## One bounded new cohort feasibility gate

Define a **separate, post-screen declared-metric frame**, not a continuation or replacement of the 62-repository frame. Before any new candidate source inspection, record a dated complete repository-search response manifest, deterministic deduplicated queue, fixed pinned commits for the bounded screened prefix, and exact adapter identity. Metadata feasibility leads collected on September 29 are not eligible projects or a frozen sample and must not be hand-selected into the cohort.

- **Primary object:** one enabled, numeric, project-owned dbt Semantic Layer metric declaration at a pinned revision, in either the legacy top-level `metrics:` form or the dbt v1.12+ model/semantic-model YAML form. `semantic_models:` entries, measures, ordinary SQL aggregate aliases, package dependency metrics, disabled metrics, and arbitrary groupings are not additional project-owned metric declarations. Track unsupported or unresolved declaration status separately and retain it in coverage counts.
- **Prospective project threshold:** at least **five** distinct inspectable primary objects, yielding at least ten unordered pairs before methods or labels. This is a study-capacity criterion, not a guarantee of positive relationships or analyzer success. Require public, independently pinned dbt source and a verified recognized open-source license governing the repository code for a distributable replication package. Record third-party dataset rights separately. Exclude documented forks, mirrors, copied templates/data of either the four development families **or the two already screened broad-frame projects**, and projects explicitly presented solely as a tutorial/example exercise; record each source-based reason before any pair analysis. Do not infer real industrial deployment from a license or README.
- **Search capture:** use the exact repository-metadata queries and API parameters in `semantic_metric_frame_precommit_20260929.json`; do not substitute the six convenience leads from the feasibility memo. Preserve complete raw responses, timestamps, parameters and hashes. Do not use candidate source snippets, metric names, pair shapes, stars, or expected method outcomes to rank. Deduplicate by numeric GitHub repository ID and sort by SHA-256 of its decimal ID string, with ID as tie-breaker. Resolve the first **20** candidate refs to a pinned commit or a terminal, evidenced unavailable status before source screening. Screen that contiguous prefix, in order, and stop at the first **three** independently eligible projects or after rank 20. Preserve every exclusion, quarantine, parse/build failure and unscreened tail. If a search page is incomplete or fails, stop before source; an individual unavailable ref remains in the prefix as an access failure and is not replaced out of order.
- **Outcome boundary:** source screen may count YAML declarations, license/family provenance and dbt project structure. It may run the unchanged MetricFlow adapter solely for coverage after selection. No metric pair comparison, relationship method, values, label, or sample draw until complete per-project declaration and pair-ID inventories are hashed, two independent qualified reviewers plus a distinct adjudicator commit a **36-person-hour** three-project feasibility budget, and identical prediction-free packets are ready. Reuse the historical lexical/lineage/hash-random eight-slot draw *per project* with empty slots retained, now for at most 24 sampled pairs; predeclare compatible query scopes in each prediction-free packet before outcomes. At 20 minutes per reviewer per pair and a 20-minute worst-case adjudication allowance, 24 pairs cost 24 person-hours; allow another 0.5 hour for three agreement audits, 6 for coordination and 5.5 contingency. The prior 24-hour/two-project sampling budget does not automatically transfer to this cohort.

If fewer than three qualifying projects occur in this **bounded search frame**, report that frame's actual flow and limits. It does not prove public Semantic Layer adoption is sparse in general. Do not keep changing the query, threshold or definition until three appear. Then decide openly whether to study SQL outputs as a separate broadened question, publish a limited feasibility result, or defer an Industrial full-paper claim. The SQL bridge may be resumed only under an explicit broader task definition with its own development tests, full source-output denominator, same-input baselines and human study; it cannot be used to relabel the two selected projects as a clean declaration-only test.

## Claim and practical-use boundary

General substitutability is a claim over the explicitly stated supported request family, not merely one ungrouped total. Any broader time-grain or dimension requests used to probe such a claim must be declared before the corresponding method/value comparison. A valid unequal keyed result at one request refutes a general claim containing that request; it does not refute an assertion explicitly limited to another scope. The Jaffle month counterexample therefore refutes the permissive AST baseline's **broad** substitution assertion on that development reconstruction, not ungrouped query identity. This scope rule is prospective for the new cohort and does not turn the development witness into held-out gold.

For the bounded **primary** pair judgment, the coordinator constructs the same prediction-free scope packet for every sampled pair after the metric-ID/pair frame and reviewer capacity are fixed, but **before** any method prediction or value execution. From pinned declarations and verified query support, choose aligned `metric_time__month` when both metrics support the same business time dimension or a source-verified mapping; otherwise choose the ungrouped scalar if both support it. If support or time alignment is unknown, retain the sampled pair with `needs_review` and name the missing prerequisite; do not try alternate dimensions to obtain an easier label. Hash the selected pair/request table, give it unchanged to both reviewers and all methods, and report month versus ungrouped coverage. This one-request primary test does not establish universal substitutability across every dimension. A method's explicitly broader assertion may be probed at additional, separately reported scopes, but an out-of-primary-scope witness is not counted as a primary-scope unsafe assertion.

Even three metric-bearing public projects and 24 independent labels would be a feasibility study. An Industrial full paper needs observed maintenance relevance, source-linked adjudicated decisions, fair baseline coverage and an honest uncertainty analysis. The public projects must not be described as enterprise deployments unless that is independently established.
